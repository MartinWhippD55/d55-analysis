# AI-DLC Assessment — Facilitator's Guide

## Before the Session

- Send the **pre-session intake form** (`intake-form.md`) at least 48 hours before. Review responses before walking in.
- Check responses against **disqualification criteria** (below). If the prospect is a bad fit, redirect to a lighter-touch alternative (webinar, content) rather than wasting the hour.
- Open the workshop HTML tool and familiarise yourself with the 8 dimensions. You don't need to memorise the questions — they're on screen — but know the flow.
- Prepare a blank notebook or shared doc for capturing notes. The HTML tool has notes fields per dimension but you'll want to capture things that don't fit the structure.

## Session Structure (60 minutes)

| Time | Activity | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| 0–5 min | **Open** | Introductions, context-set, explain the format and output. "By the end of this hour you'll have a visual picture of where you are vs where you want to be, and a clear sense of what it takes to close the gap." |
| 5–50 min | **Assessment** | Work through dimensions. ~5–6 minutes each. Use must-ask questions only; go deeper if the conversation is rich. |
| 50–55 min | **Live results** | Show the radar chart. Let it land visually. Don't oversell — let them react. |
| 55–60 min | **Close & next steps** | Summarise top 2–3 gaps. Explain engagement options briefly. Offer to send a written summary within 48 hours. |

## Running the Assessment (5–50 min)

### Pacing

- You have ~5.5 minutes per dimension. That's enough for 2–3 questions and a scoring discussion.
- **Must-ask questions** (marked with ★ in dimensions.md) are your anchors. Ask these. Always.
- **Go-deeper questions** are for when the conversation is flowing or when you sense the score is ambiguous. Skip them if time is tight.
- Watch the clock. It's better to cover all 8 shallowly than 4 deeply. The depth comes in follow-up engagements.

### Scoring in the room

- **Ask the prospect to self-score first.** "Based on what we've just discussed, where would you put yourselves — 1 to 5?" Then validate or gently challenge.
- **If there's disagreement between attendees** — that's GOLD. Note it. The disagreement IS the insight. Don't resolve it; capture it. It tells you where the internal alignment gaps are.
- **If they're between levels** — score the lower. It's more credible to slightly understate and then show progress than to overstate and have them feel the assessment was soft.
- **If they're Level 4 on one aspect but Level 2 on another within the same dimension** — score the dominant pattern. Note the split. This becomes colour commentary in the write-up.

### Facilitation tips

- **Don't interrogate.** These are conversation starters, not a checklist. Follow the energy in the room.
- **Don't sell.** If you catch yourself explaining D55's services during the assessment, stop. That's for the close. During the assessment, you're a curious, knowledgeable outsider.
- **Mirror their language.** If they say "data lake" don't correct to "lakehouse." Match their vocabulary.
- **Name the elephant.** If someone in the room looks uncomfortable or disengaged, acknowledge it. "I notice this one's landing differently for you — what's your take?" (This is the Four Fears in action.)
- **Take notes in their words.** Direct quotes are powerful in the write-up. "Our CTO said: 'honestly, we have no idea what we spend on AI'" is worth more than a paraphrased summary.

## Showing Results (50–55 min)

- Click "See Results" on the HTML tool. Let the radar chart render.
- **Pause.** Let them look at it. Don't immediately explain. The visual does the work.
- If they say "that's about right" — you've earned credibility. If they push back — explore why. Either way, you're having a productive conversation.
- Highlight the biggest gap (the dimension with the largest current-vs-target spread). "This is where the biggest opportunity lives."
- Briefly reference the engagement models section — but don't deep-dive. "There are several ways we can help close these gaps — I'll include options in the written summary."

## Closing (55–60 min)

- Thank them for their time and honesty.
- Commit to sending a written summary within 48 hours (radar chart, gap analysis, recommended next steps, engagement options).
- Ask: "Is there anyone else who should see this output?" (Expands stakeholder engagement.)
- Ask: "What would the next conversation look like for you?" (Qualifies intent without being pushy.)
- Leave the door open: "No pressure either way — the assessment is yours to keep regardless."

## After the Session

- Write up the results narrative (see template below) within 24 hours while it's fresh.
- Send the write-up plus a PDF/screenshot of the radar chart within 48 hours.
- Log the engagement in CRM.
- If strong fit: schedule follow-up within 1 week.
- If weak fit: add to nurture sequence.

## Disqualification Criteria

Do NOT run the full assessment if any of these are true:

| Signal | Why | Alternative |
|--------|-----|-------------|
| No executive sponsor AND no budget | They can't act on the output — it becomes a frustrating exercise | Offer content (webinar, blog) and revisit in 6 months |
| Fewer than 20 engineers | Engagement models don't scale down economically | Point to self-serve resources, workshops only |
| Already engaged with another consultancy on the same scope | We'd be duplicating effort and competing on a losing wicket | Ask what's not working with current partner — maybe there's an angle, but don't invest the hour speculatively |
| Not on AWS and no plans to move | Our service delivery is AWS-native; we can't credibly deliver on Azure/GCP | Be honest: "Our delivery capability is AWS-focused. If that changes, we'd love to help." |
| "Just exploring" with no trigger or deadline | No urgency = no conversion within a reasonable timeframe | Add to nurture. Send content. Check back quarterly. |

## Four Fears — Quick Reference for Facilitation

When you sense resistance in the room, diagnose which fear is at play:

| Fear | Signals | Reframe |
|------|---------|---------|
| Controller | "How do we manage this?" / "Who's accountable?" / Wants to see dashboards and metrics before anything happens | "AI gives you MORE observability, not less. Better metrics, better consistency, better oversight." |
| Driver | "What if it goes wrong?" / "What's the risk?" / Focuses on failure scenarios | "AI compresses feedback loops. You fail faster, cheaper, smaller. The real risk is slow delivery." |
| Stabiliser | "What does this mean for my team?" / "Who's going to do this?" / Focuses on org structure | "People who learn to orchestrate AI become more valuable. This strengthens your position." |
| Influencer | Goes quiet / "Where do I fit in this?" / Seems deflated by the technical conversation | "AI handles the grunt work. You get more stage for the creative, strategic, interpersonal stuff." |
