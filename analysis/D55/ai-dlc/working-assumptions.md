## WA-1 — Free assessment: stays free, paid discovery later 🟡

**Decision:** The Phase 0 assessment stays a genuine **free** diagnostic with standalone value (produces Section 0 of the manual + a radar they keep). A **paid deeper-discovery** step is introduced later, once we have proof points and demand.

**Rationale:** Builds trust and pipeline first; avoids the anchoring cliff while we have no case studies. Move to paid discovery when demand supports it.

**Affects:** commercial model, sales motion, landing-page CTA.

---

## WA-2 — Pricing model: hybrid (stage-gated entry, committed core) 🟡

**Decision:** **Hybrid.** Stage-gated entry — free Assess, per-module Teach (client picks) — then a **committed programme price** once they commit to the embed (Phase 2 + Scale).

**Rationale:** Low entry barrier lets proof build the case; the committed core captures the value where it's actually created (the embed). Maps onto the existing tiered commercial shape (Assess → Assess + Teach → Full programme).

**Affects:** commercial shape, embed scoping one-pager, proposal templates.

---

## WA-3 — External name: "AI Readiness Radar" (working) 🟡

**Decision:** Use **"AI Readiness Radar"** as the working customer-facing name.

**Rationale:** Ties to the radar artefact, memorable, was the critique's pick.

**Caveat to review with Rhys:** the name reads as the *assessment*, not the whole *programme*. Likely end state: "AI Readiness Radar" as the branded front door (Phase 0), with a programme-level wrapper name for the full transformation. Ultimately a Rhys/brand call.

**Affects:** all external collateral, positioning.md, landing page, the eyebrow on generated docs.

## WA-4 — Client Operating Manual format: generated branded document 🟡

**Decision:** The manual is a **generated branded document** (PDF/HTML), produced by a skill reusing the `deliverables-toolkit` pattern (same machinery as the programme overview doc).

**Rationale:** Cheapest, reuses existing tooling, portable. A living/editable version can come later if clients want to maintain it.

**Affects:** the future assessment→runbook skill, manual TOC deliverable format.

---

## WA-5 — Structure holds: two-runbook split + four-module clustering 🟡

**Decision:** Proceed with both structural decisions **as drafted** — the two-runbook split (internal Delivery Playbook vs Client Operating Manual) and the clustering of 8 dimensions into 4 modules.

**Rationale:** Both endorsed by the cold critique; the runbook split was its strongest verdict.

**Watch item (not a change):** the critique flagged Module 4 as carrying a lot — it bundles the Team Adaptation dimension with the hybrid-team operating model, and is the only single-dimension module. Keeping as-is for now; revisit if it strains in delivery.

**Affects:** everything — this is the spine.

## WA-6 — Embed scoping defaults (illustrative) 🟡

**Decision:** For the Phase 2 embed scoping one-pager (`embed-scoping.md`), the following working defaults apply until Rhys/delivery confirm real figures:

- **Squad ratio:** ~1:1 D55-to-client engineers; minimum viable squad 2 D55 + 2 client + 1 client PO.
- **Duration:** the 8-Week Prove-It is the *committed* proof cycle; a taper follows, scoped per client.
- **Taper shape:** full squad to wk 8 → ~50% wks 9–12 → ~25% wks 13–16 → advisory (illustrative).
- **Committed spend:** the 8-week (2-month) D55 squad cost is what the client commits to; taper quoted separately as a ramp-down.

**Rationale:** turns the embed from a rate into a quotable job. Ratio and taper are the levers that keep it capability-transfer, not staff-aug.

**Affects:** embed scoping one-pager, proposal/SOW templates, programme pricing.

**Needs delivery input:** these are the figures most in need of real-world validation from an actual embed.
