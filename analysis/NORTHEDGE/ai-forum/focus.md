## 1. People & Adoption

**The core message:** The tech is the easy part. Getting people to change is where AI transformations succeed or die — and it's exactly what failed at Cezanne (CTO moved out, pace issues).

### Four Fears Framework

| | Past/Present Leaning | Future Thinking |
|---|---|---|
| **Tasks** | Loss of Control (Controller) | Project Failure (Driver) |
| **People** | Disruption to Hierarchy (Stabiliser) | Feeling Overshadowed (Influencer) |

**The reframe — AI addresses each fear when positioned correctly:**
- Controllers get more oversight and observability, not less
- Drivers get faster, cheaper failure — compressed risk
- Stabilisers who lean in become orchestrators — more valuable
- Influencers get amplified — AI handles grunt work, they get more stage

### What We Saw (D55 Project)

- 10 developers, AI-first using Copilot + Claude (agentic tooling)
- Primary resistance came from POs, BAs, Programme Managers — the "middle layer"
- When devs go 5x faster, coordination roles can't keep pace. They become the constraint.
- Unblocked by: action first, metrics to prove it, regular retros, 1-on-1 training/support
- Not everyone came around — plan for it, budget time for it

## 2. How AI-First Changes Delivery

**The core message:** AI-first isn't "developers use Copilot." It fundamentally reshapes the lifecycle toward design-up-front. More time in analysis and specification leads to better outcomes when building with AI.

### The Lifecycle

1. **Analysis & Design** — more time spent here than traditional agile. Producing clear specifications that developers can implement with AI assistance.
2. **Contracts & Integration Points** — understanding touch points between components upfront. Engineering best practices become more important than ever.
3. **Implementation** — developers working with agentic tooling against well-defined specs.
4. **Testing** — manual testing maintained as human-in-the-loop between devs and integration points. AI used for unit tests, seed test data generation (e.g., liquibase scenarios). Investing in automated testing (Playwright MCP) for regression.
5. **Documentation & Handover** — AI useful here too. Producing assets for client handover.

### Key Insight

This isn't waterfall — it's recognising that when implementation is 5x faster, the bottleneck shifts to clarity of intent. Garbage specs at 5x speed just produces garbage faster. Design discipline is the multiplier on top of the multiplier.

## 3. The Economics

**The core message:** $200/seat/month for a 5x productivity gain. The ROI is absurd and the numbers speak for themselves.

### The Numbers

- **Tooling cost:** ~$200 per developer per month (Copilot + Claude seats)
- **Output:** 5x performance improvement, with belief this will improve further
- **For Cezanne (~100 devs):** ~$20k/month in tooling for output that would otherwise require 400+ additional developers
- **Context:** On our project, enabled a team to deliver within a tight deadline to avoid fines/penalties with the end client

### The PE Angle

- Northedge wants to improve performance and sell. This is a direct lever.
- The cost is trivial relative to developer salaries. Even at $200/seat across 100 devs ($240k/year), if it reduces headcount needs by even 20%, that's millions in savings.
- Ongoing cost is primarily tooling/seats — no expensive infrastructure or custom model training required.

## 4. Practical Starting Point ("First 8 Weeks")

**The core message:** We don't need 6 months of discovery. 8 weeks to assess, prove value, and produce a playbook.

### The Approach

**Weeks 1-2: Assessment**
- Assess the codebase and current tooling
- Understand current level of AI adoption across teams
- Identify quick wins and high-friction areas

**Weeks 3-6: Prove It**
- Embed D55 people into an existing team, or stand up a fresh D55-led squad
- Apply AI-first delivery on a real piece of work
- Track metrics from day one

**Weeks 7-8: Translate**
- Demonstrate the improvement with hard numbers
- Translate ways of working into reusable assets (guides, patterns, tooling configs)
- Produce a rollout plan for adoption by other teams

### The Model

- D55 embeds, proves, then enables. Not a permanent dependency.
- Once one squad is working, use that as the reference point to pull others forward.
- Assets and playbooks allow teams to self-serve after D55 steps back.

## 5. What "Good" Looks Like for Cezanne Specifically

**The core message:** For a PE-backed HR/payroll SaaS with ~100 devs on .NET/Aurora, "good" means doing more with the same (or fewer) people, faster release cycles, and a credible AI story for the next buyer.

### What Northedge Actually Wants

- Improve margin — do more with fewer/same people
- Increase velocity — ship faster, attract/retain customers
- Build a sellable asset — next buyer sees an AI-native engineering org, not a legacy team

### What "Good" Probably Looks Like

- 100 devs producing the output of 300-500 (5x, potentially more)
- Release cadence measured in days, not months
- AI embedded in the product (HR compliance automation, document generation, employee self-service) — adds valuation premium
- Engineering team that other acquirers see as an asset, not a cost

### What to Avoid

- Don't boil the ocean — pick 2-3 squads, prove it, roll out
- Don't start with AI product features before the engineering org is AI-native internally
- Don't treat this as a technology project — it's a people and delivery transformation that happens to use AI
