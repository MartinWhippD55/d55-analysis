# AI Forum — Summary & Analysis

## What They're Covering

1. AI → business value alignment (and failure modes)
2. Trust, adoption & governance (EU AI Act, bias, transparency)
3. Experimentation vs scalable value — what separates them
4. Pace of investment — race vs structural shift
5. Operating model, talent & culture at maturity
6. Product org design for AI (roles, lifecycle, bottlenecks)
7. Architecture & engineering patterns (agentic, RAG, fine-tuning, evals)
8. Memory/personalisation
9. Metrics & measurement
10. Competitive moat — data vs tools vs teams

## What's Missing

- **Change management & people** — no mention of reskilling existing staff, redundancy planning, or employee relations. For a PE-backed firm looking to "become smaller," this is a glaring gap.
- **Build vs buy vs partner** — no framing around when to use off-the-shelf AI products (Copilot, etc.) vs custom builds. Critical for cost control.
- **Data readiness** — they jump to architecture but skip data quality, cataloguing, lineage, and access. You can't do any of this without clean data foundations.
- **Security & threat model** — governance is covered, but adversarial attack surfaces (prompt injection, model exfiltration, supply chain risk) are absent.
- **Cost modelling** — Q5 mentions "long-term costs" in passing but there's no dedicated focus on inference costs, token economics, or total cost of ownership.
- **Customer segmentation for AI features** — which users actually benefit? No discussion of rollout strategy or feature flagging.
- **Integration with legacy systems** — Cezanne is HR/payroll software. Migration paths, API surfaces, and coexistence with legacy are unaddressed.
- **Vendor lock-in** — no discussion of model portability or multi-provider strategy.

## Our Angle

The questionnaire is heavy on "what does good look like" and light on "how do we get there from where Cezanne actually is today." That's the opening. We can position on the practical execution gap — data readiness, legacy integration, build-vs-buy decisions, cost modelling, and change management — the stuff that actually determines whether AI investments land or stall.
