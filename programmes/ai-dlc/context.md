## What this programme is

The AI Development Lifecycle programme helps engineering organisations use AI
**within their development lifecycle** — not "AI maturity" in the abstract, and not
AI product use cases. The use case *is* AI-assisted development: how a team specs,
builds, tests, ships, and governs software when developers are 3–5x faster.

It runs as one repeatable offering with a four-phase spine:

**Assess → Teach → Prove → Scale**

- **Assess** *(free)* — a ~1-hour structured assessment across 8 dimensions of
  AI-enabled engineering readiness. Output: a current-vs-target radar chart and a
  ranked, costed roadmap of what it takes to close the gap.
- **Teach** — modular workshops targeted at the gaps the assessment surfaces
  (leadership & investment case, the AI-first delivery lifecycle, shipping safely,
  people & change).
- **Prove** — an 8-week embed: D55 works inside a squad, ships real work, and tracks
  metrics from day one to prove ROI with numbers, not vibes.
- **Scale** — rollout across teams plus the Client Operating Manual, the client's
  own playbook for AI-native engineering that they own and run after D55 exits.

The free assessment is the hook. The radar gap analysis makes the paid path obvious:
each gap maps to a workshop, an embed, or a roadmap deliverable. The default first
paid step is the gap-driven Teach workshop (or, where the client wants proof before
scaling, the 8-week Prove embed).

The assessment is **no-obligation**: the client keeps the radar and the roadmap
whether or not they engage D55 further. And because the programme includes *Shipping
Safely* (governance and testing for AI-generated code), the speed it unlocks is
safe-to-ship speed, not reckless velocity.

## Why D55, why now

**Why D55:** most consultancies will help you adopt AI *tools*. We've already shipped
with AI-native teams at scale and seen what actually breaks when developers go 5x —
the bottleneck moves to specs and to the middle layer, and people resist for
personality-driven reasons, not stupidity. We diagnose the people and process
barriers that decide whether AI lands, then prove the fix in eight weeks with real
metrics. That is the difference between this and a generic AI maturity assessment:
**others score your technology; we diagnose the human and process barriers — and then
prove the fix.**

**Why now:** the triggers above are board-level. Cost pressure demands more output
per engineer; the EU AI Act sets a compliance clock; competitors are visibly
adopting; and PE/boards increasingly read AI-native engineering as a valuation
signal. The cost of waiting is measured in delayed output at 3–5x, not in tooling
spend — which makes "later" the expensive option.

## The problem we solve

Most organisations are somewhere between "a few developers use Copilot" and "we
bought enterprise licences but can't prove they're working." They feel the pressure
to adopt AI in engineering but can't answer three questions honestly:

1. Where do we actually stand today?
2. Where do we need to be, and why?
3. What will it cost — in money, time, and disruption — to get there?

The programme replaces months of internal debate with one honest conversation, a
visual of the gap, and a plan D55 is positioned to deliver.

## What makes this different (not another maturity assessment)

Four differentiators carry the programme. They are D55 IP and reappear across the
modules.

### 1. The Four Fears Framework
Most AI assessments measure technology. This one diagnoses why *people* resist
change — and what to do about it. Four personality-driven fears block adoption, and
each is answered when AI is positioned correctly:

- **Controllers** fear losing oversight → AI gives them *more* observability.
- **Drivers** fear project failure → AI compresses risk through faster feedback.
- **Stabilisers** fear hierarchy disruption → those who orchestrate AI become *more*
  valuable.
- **Influencers** fear being overshadowed → AI handles the grunt work; they get more
  stage.

### 2. The AI-First Delivery Lifecycle
The non-obvious insight: when developer productivity goes 5x, the bottleneck shifts
from *building* to *specifying*. **"Garbage specs at 5x speed = garbage 5x faster."**
More time in design, contracts defined up front, coordination roles become the
constraint — not developers.

### 3. The Middle-Layer Bottleneck
When developers go 5x faster, Product Owners, BAs and Programme Managers become the
constraint — they can't spec, break down, and coordinate at the new pace. We've seen
this firsthand and know how to address it. Most AI consultancies don't know it exists
yet because they haven't shipped with AI-native teams at scale.

### 4. The 8-Week Prove-It Model
Not twelve months of discovery. Eight weeks, three phases: **Assess** (weeks 1–2,
codebase/tooling/barriers), **Prove** (weeks 3–6, embed in a squad and ship real work
with metrics from day one), **Translate** (weeks 7–8, results in numbers, playbooks,
rollout plan). D55 embeds, proves, then enables — not a permanent dependency.

## The economics hook

AI developer tooling costs roughly £150–200 per seat per month. Observed
productivity improvement in D55 delivery experience is around 3–5x. For a
50-engineer team, that is on the order of £120k/year in tooling for output that would
otherwise need well over 100 engineers. **The real cost is delay, not investment.**

*These figures come from D55 delivery experience and are directional; they strengthen
with named case studies once available (tracked in `working-assumptions.md`).*

The assessment is **free**. The radar gap analysis drives a recommendation into one

  client's people, scoped from the gap analysis (the Prove phase).
- **Runbook & Asset Delivery** — the Client Operating Manual as the kept asset, built
  through the programme and owned by the client after D55 exits.
